Blood Choke X-Ray

What the model says happens inside the head during a blood choke, the kind that ends UFC fights. The carotid arteries are squeezed shut, blood stops reaching the front of the brain, its oxygen runs down in seconds, and it drops below the level the model treats as consciousness.

Educational model only. Not medical advice, and not for safety or training decisions. The drawing is a schematic, not a real scan, and it shows physiology, not technique.

What happened

    Chokes in UFC fights

    measured average 95% interval of that average the clock above, while the arteries are compressed

    Why every choke gets the same prediction. No study we could open measures how much each choke squeezes each artery, so the model cannot tell one from another. The fight study itself found that chokes around the neck alone and chokes with one of the fighter's arms trapped inside took the same time. The times for single choke types come from second-hand write-ups of that study, without ranges, and those write-ups report no significant difference between types. Anything that works by closing the airway instead of the arteries is outside this model.

    What is computed

    • The timing and the oxygen levels. They come from the same equation as the repo's Python model, run once for the front of the brain and once for the back.
    • How far the flow drops. It is the blood velocity measured in the brain's arteries during neck restraint (Mitchell 2012).
    • The threshold. It is fitted to 1943 data, not measured, and it is the weakest part of the model.

    What is illustration only

    • The anatomy. The shapes in the 3D view are a published 3D anatomy model of one reference adult male body (BodyParts3D, credited below), cut down to the head and neck. They are a picture only. No number in the model comes from them, and this is not a scan of any fighter. Three things were added for this page and are not from that model: plain tubes where it has no artery (between the lower and upper carotid on each side, and the lowest part of each vertebral artery), the iris, pupil and eyelids, and the leaf pattern on the cerebellum.
    • Which parts go dark. The brain is split into just two zones, a front fed by the carotids and a back fed by the vertebrals. In the picture the back is the cerebellum, the brainstem and the rearmost lobe, and each artery is coloured by which trunk it is joined to in the 3D model. That split was chosen for this page and is not a measured supply map. Real brains share supply through connecting arteries, and the model cannot say which exact regions fail first.
    • The pulses and fading arteries. Faster pulses and brighter arteries mean more flow. They are not real blood speeds, and the individual arteries are not modelled.
    • The eye. It shuts when the model's state is unconscious and opens when it is conscious again. It is a cue for that state, not a result of its own. The sign the studies timed is the eyes becoming fixed (Mitchell 2012), which the drawing does not show.
